## 1. Story diagnosis

Summarize the emotional arc in one sentence. Identify the hook, escalation, turn, climax, and final aftertaste. Preserve essential causality; compress repetition.

Translate internal states into visible behavior:

- fear → halted breath, retreating foot, fixed gaze;
- regret → touch an old object, hesitate, leave without turning;
- suspicion → stop, listen, conceal an object, watch a doorway;
- determination → settle posture, choose a direction, act without hesitation.

## 2. Runtime estimation

Estimate spoken Chinese at 4–4.5 characters per second for calm delivery and 5 characters per second only for intentionally brisk speech. Add time for breathing and reaction.

Use these starting ranges:

- one visual idea, no dialogue: 8–15s;
- simple advertisement or dance: 12–15s;
- short anecdote with one turn: 45–75s;
- folktale with escalation and reveal: 70–120s;
- dialogue scene with multiple reversals: 90–180s;
- dense article adaptation: calculate from selected narration, not source length alone.

Add time for:

- establishing a new location: 2–4s;
- readable transformation: 2–5s;
- reaction hold: 1–3s;
- complex action: 3–6s per major move;
- final image settling: 0.5–2s.

## 3. Clip splitting

Each generated clip must be 15s or shorter. Split at natural editorial boundaries:

- location or time change;
- a new objective;
- a reveal or reversal;
- before and after a transformation;
- before and after a major action impact;
- testimony versus flashback;
- setup versus final payoff.

Prefer 7–12s clips for story work. Use a full 15s when motion is continuous, such as dance, chase, performance, or a single commercial shot.

## 4. Beat design

Use 2–5 beats per clip. Weight by action, not equal duration.

Every beat must state:

- what is visible;
- subject action and screen direction;
- shot size and angle;
- one principal camera move;
- dialogue or sound at that moment.

Avoid more than one main camera move per beat. Reserve a stable hold for the final 0.5–1s when possible.

## 5. Camera and editing

Choose one dominant grammar per clip:

- continuous push or orbit for emotional, performance, and reveal shots;
- cut-driven coverage for trailers and dense action;
- handheld for urgency and documentary realism;
- locked-off for uncanny or comic timing;
- parallel tracking for running, walking, vehicles, or lateral combat;
- low ground tracking for speed and scale;
- overhead withdrawal for aftermath and helplessness.

Maintain screen direction across adjacent clips unless a deliberate axis break communicates disorientation.

## 6. Spoken-time discipline

Do not duplicate the same information in dialogue and narration. Alternate their jobs:

- narration supplies time, context, or compressed exposition;
- dialogue supplies immediate intention, conflict, or discovery;
- silence supplies suspense, grief, wonder, and comic timing.

If dialogue plus narration exceeds the clip, remove one, shorten it, or split the clip. Never solve this by forcing unnaturally fast speech.
